## 1. The Race in a Nutshell

Justice of the Peace is a low-information, down-ballot race. Most voters will not know either candidate's name when they reach that line on the ballot. That means the race is won on **name recognition and personal trust**, not policy. The candidate whose name voters have seen in yards, at church, and on their neighbor's Facebook page wins.

Turnout in a JP ward race is small — often only a few thousand votes cast, and margins of a few hundred. **Every handshake genuinely matters.**

### Winning message (keep it to one sentence)
> "Mark Booth — a fair, honest neighbor who'll treat everyone in Ward 2 with respect."

Repeat it everywhere. Don't complicate it.

- [ ] Election Day: volunteers with signs at (legal distance from) the busiest Ward 2 polling places; Mark visits polls all day shaking hands

## 5. Three Rules for a Local Race

1. **The candidate is the campaign.** Mark's face and handshake beat every ad. Schedule his time like it's the budget's biggest line item — because it is.
2. **Ask directly.** For votes, for signs, for money, for shares. People say yes to direct personal asks and ignore general appeals.
3. **Never go negative.** In a race this small, everybody knows everybody. Stay likable.
